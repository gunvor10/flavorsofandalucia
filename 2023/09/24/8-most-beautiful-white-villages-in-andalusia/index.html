8 most beautiful white villages in Andalusia

Gunvor Guttormsen 24 September 2023 8 min read

Between Cádiz and Málaga, in the Andalusian mountains, lie some of the region's most valuable treasures.

Whitewashed villages with labyrinths of cobbled streets, decorated with the colourful flowers of Andalusia. This is where time stands still and where you find the quiet you were looking for. You can walk among pinsapo firs, take in extraordinary landscapes, follow good hiking routes, and read the traces of the many civilisations that have lived here.

Here are eight of the most striking white villages to see while you are in Andalucía, and what each one is known for.

In short

  • —Most sit between Cádiz and Málaga, in the Sierra de Cádiz and the Serranía de Ronda.
  • —Ronda is the one reachable by public transport; the rest realistically need a car.
  • —Grazalema is the wettest place in Spain, which is why these mountains are green.
  • —Setenil's houses are built in under an overhanging cliff, so the rock forms the roof.

01

Ronda

Ronda, where the long Andalusian history has left proof of many different civilisations, with traces running back to Roman times. The landscape, the extraordinary cliff, the romantic legend of its bandits, and its lineage of bullfighters and artists are what make it remarkable.

It has inspired a long line of writers and poets — Pliny, al-Mutamid the poet-king of Seville, al-Idrisi, Ibn al-Khatib and Rilke among them, all captivated by the charm of its streets and corners. Walk the narrow lanes along the edge of the cliff and across the Puente Nuevo, stopping at every peculiar corner of the old town.

Serranía de Ronda1 h 45 from SevilleReachable by train and bus

02

Setenil de las Bodegas

Setenil is one of those villages whose unusual character makes the Sierra de Cádiz one of the most picturesque regions in Spain. It has been settled since ancient times, below the castle and along the river Guadalporcún.

Part of the route of the white villages of the Sierra de Cádiz, its caves and natural recesses in the rock sheltered its first inhabitants; later the caves became houses. Today it is one of the most visited villages in the province, a member of the association of the Most Beautiful Towns of Spain, and its urban structure is a protected historic ensemble.

03

Grazalema

The highest village in the Sierra de Cádiz, and the wettest place in Spain. It sits inside the Sierra de Grazalema natural park, declared a biosphere reserve in 1977, and El Torreón, the highest peak in Cádiz province, draws hikers of every level.

It is one of the most charming villages in the province and the finest example of the type, proud of its Roman and Moorish past — first known by the Moorish name Raisa lani suli, 'city of the Banu al-Salim', which became Ben-salama and later Gran Zulema.

Most people here still work in agriculture, producing goat's cheese, excellent olive oil and handwoven textiles, alongside the tourism drawn by the landscape of the natural park.

04

Zahara de la Sierra

White houses stacked in a steep cone beneath a ruined Moorish castle, with a turquoise reservoir spread out below. It is the image most people have in mind when they say pueblo blanco.

The castle was taken by Christian forces in 1483, and that act triggered the final campaign against Granada. The village is a natural pairing with Grazalema — the road between the two crosses the Puerto de las Palomas pass, which is worth the drive on its own.

05

Frigiliana

Frigiliana's roots run back to Roman times, but it was the Muslim settlement after the eleventh-century invasion that gave the village its shape, protected by a fortress. It stands 300 metres above sea level with wide views over Nerja and the Costa del Sol, and on a clear winter day you can see the North African coast from its position in the Sierra Almijara.

Walking its streets is worth every minute: cobbles laid in intricate stone patterns, whitewashed walls hung with flowers, and in the upper village the old Moorish quarter, where doors are painted in pastel colours and gaps between the houses open onto views of the Mediterranean and the countryside. Writers and poets have found their inspiration here, and it is regularly named among the prettiest villages in Andalucía.

Province of Málaga300 m above sea levelViews to North Africa

06

Arcos de la Frontera

Arcos sits in the province of Cádiz, and its history is best read by walking the narrow streets of the old town, declared a Monument of Historical and Artistic Importance in 1962. It is described in more than one guide as the most beautiful town in Spain.

The town stands on top of a rock between mountains and open country, with commanding views in every direction. The castle and the churches of Santa María and San Pedro are the great monuments, found among the endless corners of the whitewashed labyrinth.

For anyone looking for quiet it is a favourite: relaxed, away from the noise, and within easy reach of the main cities of western Andalucía and the Atlantic beaches. It has also become a popular place for traditional weddings.

Province of CádizProtected since 19621 h 15 from Seville

07

Casares

Casares is one of the finest of the hilltop villages, set between the Serranía de Ronda, the Costa del Sol and the Campo de Gibraltar, with panoramic views down to the Mediterranean and across to the North African coast.

It is a protected historic village. Its roots are Roman, but the village as it stands is Arabic in character. The ruins of the twelfth-century Moorish castle crown it, and excavations show Casares was important as one of the forts between the coast of the Strait and the Ronda mountains. It is also the birthplace of Blas Infante, the writer and politician considered the father of Andalusian nationalism.

Province of Málaga12th-century castleBirthplace of Blas Infante

08

Olvera

Olvera is the gateway to the pueblos blancos route in the Sierra de Cádiz. Whitewashed houses and alleys climb the hillside among olive groves, crowned by the historic castle and the old quarter raised in Nasrid times.

The streets of the historic centre twist across the slope, holding the scent of the plants that cover the walls and windows — popular architecture in the classic Andalusian style. Above it stands the twelfth-century Moorish castle, silent witness to the town's history, and the church towers rise beside it.

The setting is the far north-east of the Betic range, among rounded hills and fields of cereal and olive. The highlight of this sierra is the Peñón de Zaframagón, a nature reserve holding the largest concentration of griffon vultures anywhere in Andalusia.

Sierra de CádizGateway to the routePeñón de Zaframagón

Seeing them without spending the day in a car

The villages are scattered, and public transport between them barely exists — one bus a day in places, none in others, and no useful connections. Ronda you can reach by train or coach from Seville; the rest need a car or an organised trip.

The route that works in a single day from Seville is Zahara, Grazalema, Ronda and Setenil, which comes to roughly five hours of driving in total. Two villages and a long lunch makes a better day than four and a schedule.

Frigiliana and Casares sit much further east, near the Costa del Sol, and belong to a trip based in Málaga or Marbella rather than a day out from Seville. Tell us what you want from the day and we will build it around that.
